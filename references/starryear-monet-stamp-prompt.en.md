# S-004 · Starryear-Monet-Stamp — Full Prompt

Treat the photograph uploaded in this turn as the **primary and sole visual evidence**. Create one finished vertical 2:3 travel-archive artwork with three fundamentally different states: top **PHOTOGRAPHIC EVIDENCE**, middle **SYMBOLIC IMPRINT**, and bottom **MONET MEMORY**. The method is fixed; the visual template is not.

## 1. Location and scene identity

Location accuracy outranks design completeness. A country, city, site, building, or attraction supplied by the user has highest priority; lock it unless the photograph clearly contradicts it, and never silently add a more specific level. If the user gives no location, inspect readable signage, official inscriptions, station or road names, unique landmark structures, and multiple independent structural clues.

Use only the geographic level supported by evidence. Do not complete blurred text. Style, atmosphere, or resemblance to a famous destination is a hypothesis, not evidence; a model-generated candidate cannot later support itself. When identity remains uncertain, switch to scene-based archive mode and create a truthful observable title such as `WATERSIDE GARDEN STUDY` or `LAKESIDE BOAT STUDY`. Perform a second independent precision check immediately before rendering any geographic name.

## 2. Internal visual analysis

Do not display the analysis. Identify primary subjects and approximate counts, dominant visual mass, 3–7 recognizable clues, 2–4 repeated or structural relationships, geometry, direction, foreground/middle/background, density, negative space, and the source dominant, secondary, midtone, shadow, highlight, and genuine accent colors.

## 3. Canvas and material

Use a vertical 2:3 canvas: top 38–42%, middle 25–28%, bottom 30–35%. Slight overlap through torn fibers, pigment, or print seams is allowed, but the states must stay distinct. Use warm matte ivory paper, subtle newsprint grain, uneven absorption, broken letterpress edges, minor folds, and slight misregistration. Keep 80–90% clean and breathable; concentrate distress at selected edges, seams, folds, and postmarks. Exclude all-over gray dirt, coffee stains, soot, dense scratches, heavy grain, and random splatter.

## 4. Top — Photographic Evidence

This is the only region allowed to preserve the original photographic viewpoint. Faithfully preserve architecture, people, animals, boats, vehicles, vegetation, water, skyline, lighting, perspective, placement, and spatial relationships. Do not reconstruct architecture, replace subjects, add famous landmarks, remove major objects, or dramatically recolor. Allow only restrained archival reproduction: slight fading, softened blacks, very fine halftone and film grain, tiny scratches, edge oxidation, and uneven absorption. It must remain photographic—not cinematic, HDR, heavily sepia, painterly, or regenerated scenery.

## 5. Middle — Symbolic Imprint

Select 2–4 relationships from the strongest source motif, destroy the original perspective, and compress them into one dominant symbol, one secondary rhythm, and one or two tiny marks. Leave 55–70% exposed paper. Keep it sparse, concentrated, asymmetrical, and away from centered logo symmetry. Roofs may become displaced wedges, arches broken apertures, trunks vertical cuts, water reflection bands, or boat canopies floating arcs—but the actual choice must come from this photo.

Use broken ink, uneven pressure, dry-brush edges, woodblock texture, missing pigment, minor bleeding, and small registration shifts, normally with 2–4 source-derived pigment groups. Do not illustrate the whole scene, miniaturize the top, trace objects, or create clean vectors. Never default to a red sun, moon, halo, disc, pagoda, pavilion, mountain, or generic landmark. Chinese and English may appear in the middle, but geographic text must pass the identity rule; use a truthful scene name when unknown.

## 6. Bottom — Monet Memory

Select about 4–7 important source fragments and **disassemble and reconstruct** them as a new painterly memory. Enlarge, crop, move, repeat, overlap, detach reflections, compress background, expand vegetation, flatten depth, or dissolve areas into paper. Do not preserve the original framing, placement, or photographic perspective, but preserve the dominant subject's visual weight. Use one large dominant structure, 2–4 medium rhythms, a few directional marks, an entry point, a concentration zone, and a quiet breathing area.

Use the perceptual logic of Impressionist landscape painting: broken color, reflected light, soft atmospheric distance, controlled visible brushwork, selective edge loss, color vibration, and luminous midtones. Do not copy any specific Monet painting, cover the section in heavy impasto, create muddy sludge, become excessively romantic, or apply an oil-paint filter. Painterly treatment should chiefly affect sky, water, vegetation, reflections, atmosphere, and selected architecture while some structural fragments remain sharper.

Discover color from the photograph: approximately 70–85% source dominant, secondary, midtone, and shadow colors; 10–25% source highlights and atmospheric tones; 0–10% slightly intensified genuine source accents. Preserve at least three recognizable source color groups when available. Never impose sepia, rust, black-gold, mustard, red-green, blue-orange, or another stock vintage palette.

## 7. English information field and postmark

Let the painterly memory occupy roughly two-thirds to three-quarters of the bottom width. Place a narrow aged-paper information field on the right with a slightly torn or dissolved edge and limited picture overlap. **All typography in the bottom—including titles, metadata, specimen numbers, and postmark—must be English only.**

For a user-supplied or verified site, use `[SITE]`, `[CITY] · [COUNTRY IF KNOWN]`, one short reliable factual sentence, and `SPECIMEN NO. [NUMBER]`. If only the city is reliable, use only that city and a reliable country. If location is unverified, use a 2–5-word observable scene title, `LOCATION UNCONFIRMED` or `SITE UNVERIFIED`, one sentence describing only visible elements, and a specimen number. Never invent dates, dynasties, architects, construction years, heritage status, historical events, or cultural significance.

Place one large circular postal cancellation across the artwork-field boundary, about 45–55% on each side, as if physically stamped after assembly. Use English only. A confirmed identity uses confirmed levels only; an unknown site uses its scene title or neutral wording such as `TRAVEL FIELD ARCHIVE`, `FIELD OBSERVATION`, or `ARCHIVE SPECIMEN`. Derive the central icon from the current photo and compress it into about 2–6 engraved strokes. Use a broken circumference, missing ink, faded letters, slight rotation, minor double printing, bleeding, and 3–5 faded cancellation waves. Exclude perfect digital circles, logo symmetry, and fixed icons.

## 8. Final check and output

Confirm that user identity was respected; no hypothesis became fact; geographic precision is supported; the middle symbol and stamp icon come from this photo; no fixed decoration appeared; at least three source color groups survive where possible; the dominant visual weight remains; the bottom is genuinely reconstructed, luminous, and clean; the bottom contains no Chinese; no fact was invented; and the states clearly read `EVIDENCE → IMPRINT → MONET MEMORY`.

Return exactly **one finished vertical 2:3 image**. Do not return analysis, alternatives, prompt notes, extra copy, mockup framing, logos, signatures, or watermarks.
